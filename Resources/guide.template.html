Setting up proDOCKtivity

About 5 minutes. No tech knowledge needed. Just follow the steps in order.

What is it?

proDOCKtivity is a bar on the left side of your screen. It keeps your apps, websites and folders in one tidy place, sorted into “workspaces” (for example Uni, Work, Free time). Click something and it opens right next to the bar.

You need
  • A Mac with an Apple chip (M1 or newer). To check: Apple menu (top left) → About This Mac → “Chip”.
  • macOS 27 or newer.
  • No extra browser needed: websites open in proDOCKtivity's own browser window. If you prefer, you can use Google Chrome or Arc instead (see “Choosing a browser” below).

Install

  1. Unpack the file

    You got a file called proDOCKtivity.zip. Double-click it. A proDOCKtivity app appears next to it.

  2. Move it into “Applications”

    Open a new Finder window, click Applications on the left, and drag proDOCKtivity into it.

  3. Open it the first time

    Double-click proDOCKtivity in Applications. macOS shows a message that it can't check the app. That's normal, because the app isn't from the App Store. Click Done.

    1. Open the Apple menu (top left) → System Settings → Privacy & Security.
    2. Scroll down to Security. Next to “proDOCKtivity was blocked…”, click Open Anyway.
    3. Enter your Mac password, then click Open Anyway once more.

    Only once. After this, proDOCKtivity opens normally.

  4. The setup tour (about a minute)

    On the first start a small window offers three ways to set up:

    • Quick setup (recommended): good settings and three workspaces, Uni, Work and Free time. One click.
    • Step by step: four short questions: which browser, your workspaces (or bring them over from Arc, Chrome or Safari bookmarks), how windows behave, and two permissions.
    • From a setup file: a setupWA or setupNA file from another Mac or a friend (see “Another Mac, backups” below).

    Everything can be changed later in Settings. To run the tour again: ⋯ (bottom left of the bar) → Setup Tour…

Allow what it needs

  1. Accessibility (to place windows next to the bar)

    1. In the setup tour, click Allow… next to “Snap windows next to the bar”. Or, in the bar, click Open Accessibility Settings… on the card “Snap windows next to the bar”. If a message “proDOCKtivity would like to control this computer” appears, click Open System Settings.
    2. In the list, switch proDOCKtivity on. If it's not in the list: click +, choose Applications → proDOCKtivity → Open, then switch it on.
    3. Enter your Mac password if asked.

  2. Chrome or Arc (only if you use one of them)

    When a message says “proDOCKtivity wants to control Google Chrome” (or Arc), click Allow. If you clicked “Don't Allow” by mistake: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation → proDOCKtivity → switch on Google Chrome or Arc.

  3. Downloads (optional)

    If asked whether proDOCKtivity may access your Downloads folder, click Allow. Then the ↓ button at the bottom of the bar shows your recent downloads.

Using it

Your workspaces sit as icons at the bottom of the bar; click one, or swipe with two fingers on the bar, to switch. At the top are your Favorites (apps and websites you use everywhere), below them the workspace's own pinned apps, websites and folders, then what's open right now. To tidy up, press ⌃ control ⌥ option W (Show All Workspaces): every workspace side by side, drag things from one to another. Right-click anything in the bar to see what you can do with it. Drag things to sort them.

Windows next to the bar

Apps and websites you open from the bar fill the space next to it (this needs Accessibility, step 5).

The frame: everything in one window

Like Arc, everything looks like one window: your workspace's color sits behind everything, with a thin frame around the bar and your windows, all in one piece. Every window snaps inside the frame, also new ones and ones you bring forward; a smaller window that's already inside stays as it is. Free to break out: apps set to “Don't snap” (Finder, Terminal), full-screen apps and windows that can't be resized. Where your screen meets a second monitor there's no frame. In Settings → Windows → Frame you set the width, turn it off, and choose whether it stops below the menu bar (rounded top corners) or reaches up behind it.

Docks: websites and files next to the bar

In Settings → Bar & Docks → Docks: Websites in a dock of their own puts the workspace's websites and tabs in a second bar right of the bar; the bar keeps your favorites and apps. The Files dock (left next to the bar, or at the right screen edge) is a small Finder: add folders with +, click a file to open it, drag files into Mail or an upload. Right-click a folder → Starts open to have it open from the start.

Freeze one thing on one half

Hold ⌃ control and click an app or a website (it goes to the left half), or right-click it → Freeze on the Left or Freeze on the Right. It stays on that half (it gets a small blue snowflake), and everything you open after it goes to the other half. Example: freeze your calendar on the right, then click through your tabs, and they all show on the left. To stop, ⌃ control-click it again, or right-click → Release Freeze. (A split with ⌥ option is different: it ends as soon as you click something else.)

Where an app's new windows go

Right-click an app → New Windows: fill the space, right half, left half, small in the middle, or don't snap. With Only extra windows, the app's main window stays as it is and only further windows follow the rule. Already set up: Finder and Terminal don't snap; in Mail, a new email opens on the right half next to your inbox. All of these rules are listed in Settings → Windows → Where each app's windows go, where you can also add an app or set one back.

Tidying up automatically

Settings → Auto-Quit. Under Open apps and tabs choose after how long apps you haven't used are hidden or quit (for example 30 minutes) and when old tabs close. Favorites and every workspace can have their own rule: keep their apps open, hide or quit them after another time, and close their tabs sooner, later or never. Then add exceptions that win wherever they are: Add App Exception… lists all your apps, and under Exceptions for websites you type a site such as gmail.com so its tabs are never closed. The app you're using, apps in a call and pinned websites in a workspace are never touched; ⌘ Y brings back what was closed.

The built-in browser

Click a website in the bar and it opens in proDOCKtivity's own browser window, right next to the bar. Each workspace keeps its own open tabs. Like in Arc, the window is just the page: the address, back, forward and reload sit at the top of the bar (back and forward only show when there's somewhere to go), and the red/yellow/green buttons sit in a small notch in the page's top-left corner. The red button only hides the window; your tabs stay open and come back when you click a website again.

The address is in the bar's top-left corner: click it or press ⌘ L, type, press Enter. (If you'd rather have it in the menu bar after the menus: Settings → Browser.) Searching in DOCKlight only switches to a tab of the workspace you're in; a site that's open in another workspace opens here instead. Copy, paste, select all, undo, find (⌘ F) work as in any browser. Two tabs side by side: while the browser is in front, ⌥ option-click another tab; the one you were on goes left, the new one right.

Profiles: who is logged in where

Logins (Gmail, Instagram, your uni account …) are stored in a profile. Each workspace uses one profile, and several workspaces can share one. Example: a “Private” profile for both “Free time” and “Uni”, and a “Work” profile just for “Work”. Then you're logged in to your private accounts in two workspaces, and to your work accounts only in one.

  1. Right-click a workspace icon at the bottom of the bar → Profile: …
  2. Pick an existing profile, or choose New Profile… and give it a name.
  3. Log in to your websites once in that workspace. Every workspace with the same profile is then logged in too.

At the start there is one profile, “Private”, used by every workspace. In the same menu you can also rename a profile, sign out of everything in it, or delete it.

Links from other apps (default browser)

So that links you click in Mail, Slack, WhatsApp and other apps open in proDOCKtivity too: Settings → Browser → Make proDOCKtivity the Default Browser, then confirm with Use proDOCKtivity. Links then open as a tab in the workspace you're in.

Extensions (ad blocker, Zotero …)

Settings → Browser → Extensions. Get uBlock Origin Lite adds the ad and tracker blocker with one click. Found on this Mac lists extensions you can take over, for example the Zotero Connector from the Zotero app, or extensions you have in Chrome or Arc. Add from File… takes a folder, .zip, .xpi or .crx. Each extension has a switch and a trash can. Their buttons (for example Zotero's “Save to Zotero”) are behind the puzzle piece 🧩 next to reload at the top of the bar.

Passwords

Apple's Passwords app can't fill in logins here: macOS only lets Safari, Chrome, Edge and Firefox talk to it. Open the Passwords app, copy the password (right-click → Copy Password), click into the website's password field and paste with ⌘ V. You usually only need to do this once per profile, because logins are remembered.

Search engine

What you type that isn't an address is searched with Google. To use DuckDuckGo, Ecosia, Bing or Startpage instead: Settings → Browser → Search with.

Choosing a browser

⋯ (bottom left of the bar) → Browser (or Settings → Browser). Built-in is the default. Pick Google Chrome if you need an extension that doesn't work here (for example Claude in Chrome or Apple's Passwords autofill); websites then open in Chrome, using Chrome's own logins (profiles don't apply there). Arc works as well.

Another Mac, backups, updates

Setup files set up proDOCKtivity on another Mac in one go. setupNA (settings, workspaces and websites, no apps) is saved by itself after every change; setupWA also holds your apps and their rules. Settings → Data → Setup file saves or loads them; logins, open tabs and ideas are never in them.

Backups: a copy of your data is made every day and kept for two weeks (Settings → Data → Backups). Nothing leaves your Mac: no account, no sync.

Updates: when a new version is out, a card at the bottom of the bar offers to update, and proDOCKtivity restarts by itself (Settings → General → Updates).

Sending your ideas to Bennet

Everything you note with ⌃ ⌥ I is collected in one text file. To send it: ⋯ (bottom left of the bar) → Ideas & Wishes → Email Ideas to Bennet…. Mail opens with the file attached. Or choose Export Ideas File… and send it however you like.

If something doesn't work

Windows don't move next to the bar

Accessibility isn't switched on. Do step 5 again. If proDOCKtivity is already in the list, switch it off and on again.

A website says I'm logged out

Logins belong to the workspace's profile. Right-click the workspace icon → Profile and check that it's the profile you logged in with, or log in once more in this workspace.

My Chrome or Arc tabs don't show up

Open that browser once. Then check step 6 (Automation → Google Chrome or Arc).

The bar is gone

Press ⌃ ⌥ S. If that doesn't help, open proDOCKtivity again from Applications.

I want to quit it

Click the bar icon in the menu bar at the top right → Quit proDOCKtivity.
